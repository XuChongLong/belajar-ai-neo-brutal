# AI Engineering Curriculum Research Notes

## Source hierarchy

The user-provided PDF of **Chip Huyen, _AI Engineering: Building Applications with Foundation Models_** is the required primary source for the new track. The user-provided YouTube speedrun is a secondary onboarding and recap aid only. The curriculum will explain ideas in original Indonesian wording and will not reproduce the book’s prose, exercises, diagrams, or code.

## Primary-source learning sequence

The book describes an end-to-end workflow for adapting foundation models to real applications. Its high-level chapter sequence provides the curriculum spine:

| Book chapter | Curriculum focus |
|---|---|
| 1 | Deciding whether an AI application is worth building, its use case, and its constraints |
| 2 | How foundation models behave: data, architecture, training, post-training, generation, and parameters |
| 3–4 | Evaluation foundations and a dependable evaluation pipeline |
| 5 | Prompt engineering, prompt attacks, and defenses |
| 6 | Context construction through RAG and agentic patterns |
| 7 | Finetuning trade-offs, efficient adaptation, and model merging |
| 8 | Data acquisition, annotation, synthesis, processing, and quality |
| 9 | Faster and cheaper inference at the model and serving layers |
| 10 | End-to-end system design, monitoring, and useful user-feedback loops |

## Secondary speedrun cues

The speedrun is useful as an orientation map: it moves through model behavior, evaluation, model selection, prompts, RAG, agents, finetuning, data, inference, architecture, and feedback. It will be represented by optional **“Speedrun checkpoint”** cues rather than treated as canonical content. Its practical reminders—start with prompts, version prompts, validate agent plans before actions, log inputs and outputs, and connect technical evaluation to product outcomes—are compatible with the primary workflow but will be framed with production caveats.

## Teaching constraints

Each lesson should be small, practical, and friendly in Indonesian. The narrator may use a warm address such as **“beb”** when it feels natural, but should not overuse it. Lessons will explain concepts with original examples, hands-on prompts, short quizzes, and links to official documentation where appropriate.
